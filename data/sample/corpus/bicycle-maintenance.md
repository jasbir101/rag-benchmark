# Basic bicycle maintenance

A few minutes of regular care keeps a bicycle safe and efficient. The three things worth checking most often are tyre pressure, the chain and the brakes.

Tyres slowly lose air even without a puncture. Check pressure weekly with a gauge rather than by squeezing the tyre. The acceptable range is printed on the tyre sidewall. Road tyres typically run between 80 and 100 psi, while mountain bike tyres run much lower, often 25 to 35 psi, for grip on loose ground. Underinflated tyres feel sluggish and are prone to pinch flats, where the tube is squeezed against the rim.

The chain needs cleaning and lubrication. Wipe it with a rag, apply one drop of chain lube to each roller while turning the pedals backwards, then wipe off the excess. Lube left on the outside of the chain only attracts grit. Wet lube suits rainy conditions; dry lube suits dusty ones.

Chains stretch as their pins and rollers wear. A chain checker tool shows when wear reaches about 0.5 to 0.75 percent, which is the point to replace it. Replacing a worn chain early protects the far more expensive cassette and chainrings.

Rim brake pads wear down and should be replaced when the grooves disappear. Disc brakes squeal when the rotor or pads are contaminated with oil; cleaning the rotor with isopropyl alcohol often fixes it. If the brake lever pulls all the way to the handlebar, the cable needs tightening or hydraulic brakes need bleeding.

Finally, check that the quick-release levers or thru-axles are closed firmly before every ride. A wheel that comes loose is far more dangerous than any worn part.
