# Caring for houseplants

Most houseplants that fail indoors are overwatered rather than neglected. Roots need air as well as water, and soil that stays soggy suffocates them, leading to root rot. The safest habit is to water thoroughly, let excess drain from the bottom of the pot, and then wait until the top two or three centimetres of soil feel dry before watering again.

Pots without drainage holes make overwatering almost inevitable. If a decorative container has no hole, keep the plant in a plastic nursery pot inside it and empty any water that collects at the bottom.

Light is the other major factor. Plants described as "low light" tolerate dim corners but grow slowly there. Succulents and cacti need several hours of direct sun, ideally from a south- or west-facing window in the northern hemisphere. Leggy growth that stretches towards the window is a sign that a plant wants more light; scorched, bleached patches on leaves suggest too much direct sun.

Humidity indoors often drops in winter when heating runs. Tropical plants such as calatheas and ferns react with crispy brown leaf edges. Grouping plants together or placing them on a tray of wet pebbles raises humidity a little; misting has only a brief effect.

Repot when roots circle the inside of the pot or grow out of the drainage holes, usually every one to two years. Choose a pot only a few centimetres wider than the old one, because a much larger volume of soil stays wet for too long.

Fertilise lightly during spring and summer, when plants are actively growing, and stop in winter. Yellowing lower leaves can mean a lack of nitrogen, but they are also a normal sign of old leaves being shed.
