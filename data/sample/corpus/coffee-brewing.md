# Brewing better coffee

Brewing coffee is the controlled extraction of soluble compounds from roasted, ground beans. The main variables are the ratio of coffee to water, grind size, water temperature and contact time.

A good starting ratio for filter coffee is about 1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water, for example 20 grams of coffee for 320 grams of water. Weighing with a kitchen scale is far more consistent than measuring with scoops, because beans vary in density.

Grind size controls how fast water extracts flavour. Finer grounds expose more surface area and extract faster. Espresso needs a fine grind because water passes through in under 30 seconds. A French press uses a coarse grind because the coffee steeps for around four minutes and a fine grind would slip through the mesh filter.

Taste tells you how to adjust. Under-extracted coffee tastes sour, salty or thin; grind finer or brew longer. Over-extracted coffee tastes bitter, harsh and drying; grind coarser or shorten the brew.

Water just off the boil, between 90 and 96 degrees Celsius, works well for most methods. Water quality matters too: coffee is more than 98 percent water, so a strongly chlorinated or very hard supply will flatten the cup. Filtered water is a simple improvement.

Freshness has a large effect. Whole beans keep their aroma for a few weeks after roasting when stored in an airtight container away from light and heat. Ground coffee goes stale within hours, so grinding just before brewing is the single biggest upgrade for most people. Avoid storing beans in the refrigerator, where they pick up moisture and odours.
