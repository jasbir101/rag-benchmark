# Training for a first marathon

A typical first-marathon plan lasts 16 to 20 weeks and assumes the runner can already run comfortably for 30 to 45 minutes. The plan gradually increases weekly distance so that muscles, tendons and bones can adapt.

The long run is the backbone of the plan. Once a week, usually at the weekend, the runner covers a steadily increasing distance at an easy, conversational pace. Most plans peak with one or two long runs of 29 to 32 kilometres about three to four weeks before race day. Running the full 42.2 kilometres in training is rarely recommended because the recovery cost is high.

A common guideline is to increase total weekly distance by no more than about ten percent from one week to the next, with an easier "cutback" week every three or four weeks. Most injuries in marathon training come from doing too much too soon rather than from any single run.

Easy running should make up the large majority of training. Many plans add one quality session per week, such as tempo runs at a comfortably hard pace or intervals, to improve speed and lactate threshold.

Fuelling is practised in training, not invented on race day. For runs longer than about 90 minutes, most runners take energy gels or sports drinks every 30 to 45 minutes. Trying the same products in training avoids stomach trouble during the race.

The final two to three weeks are the taper: weekly distance drops while some intensity is kept, so the body can recover and arrive at the start line fresh. Many runners feel restless during the taper, but extra training at this stage cannot build fitness in time and only adds fatigue.
